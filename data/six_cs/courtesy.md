# Courtesy

## Definition
Courtesy in technical writing refers to the use of polite, respectful, and reader-centered language that promotes goodwill and cooperation. Courteous writing considers the reader’s perspective and avoids language that may sound harsh, demanding, or antagonistic.

Courtesy improves tone, not content. It should never override clarity, conciseness, or accuracy.

## Key Principles
- Use polite expressions when appropriate.
- Avoid commanding or aggressive language unless required.
- Show respect for the reader’s role and time.
- Maintain a professional and neutral tone.

## Common Courtesy Problems

### Abrupt or Commanding Tone
Using language that sounds forceful or demanding.

Examples:
- “Submit the report immediately.”
- “Fix this issue now.”

### Lack of Softening Language
Omitting polite markers when making requests.

Examples:
- Missing “please”
- Missing acknowledgment or appreciation

### Antagonistic or Dismissive Language
Using wording that may create tension or defensiveness.

Examples:
- “You failed to submit the document.”
- “This was done incorrectly.”

## Examples

### Poor (Abrupt)
Submit the report immediately.

### Improved
Please submit the report at your earliest convenience.

---

### Poor (Harsh)
You failed to follow the instructions.

### Improved
The instructions were not followed as outlined.

---

### Poor (Demanding)
Fix the error before the meeting.

### Improved
Please correct the error before the meeting.

## When to Apply Courtesy
Apply courtesy when:
- The sentence sounds harsh or demanding
- The message is a request, reminder, or instruction
- The wording may affect professional relationships
- Politeness can improve cooperation

## When NOT to Apply Courtesy
Do NOT apply courtesy when:
- The sentence is purely informational
- Adding politeness would reduce clarity
- The context requires firm or direct instruction
- The tone is already appropriate

## Diagnostic Questions
Ask these questions to test courtesy:
- Does the sentence sound overly commanding?
- Would a polite marker improve cooperation?
- Is the tone appropriate for a professional setting?
- Would adding politeness change the intent of the message?
