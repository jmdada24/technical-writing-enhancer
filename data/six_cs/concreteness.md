# Concreteness

## Definition
Concreteness in technical writing refers to the use of specific, precise, and tangible language that allows the reader to clearly visualize and understand the message. Concrete writing avoids vague, abstract, or general terms when specific details can improve clarity and comprehension.

Concrete language answers the reader’s natural questions and reduces the need for follow-up clarification.

## Key Principles
- Use specific nouns instead of general ones.
- Use precise verbs instead of vague actions.
- Provide measurable or observable details when relevant.
- Replace abstract descriptions with tangible information.

## Common Concreteness Problems

### Vague Nouns
Using general words that do not clearly describe what is being referred to.

Examples:
- “thing”
- “stuff”
- “equipment”
- “device”

### Abstract Descriptions
Using concepts or ideas without physical or observable references.

Examples:
- “improve performance”
- “increase efficiency”
- “enhance quality”

### Missing Specifics
Leaving out details that would help the reader visualize or act.

Examples:
- Size
- Quantity
- Color
- Type
- Location

## Examples

### Poor (Vague)
I bought a dog yesterday.

### Improved
I bought a brown-and-white male St. Bernard puppy yesterday.

---

### Poor (Abstract)
The system experienced issues during operation.

### Improved
The system shut down twice during the installation process.

---

### Poor (Missing Details)
Please submit the report soon.

### Improved
Please submit the report by Friday at 3 p.m.

## Example Dialogue (Illustrating Concreteness)

Person 1: I bought a dog yesterday.  
Person 2: What kind?  
Person 1: A St. Bernard puppy, male, brown and white.

### Improved Statement
I bought a brown-and-white male St. Bernard puppy yesterday.

## When to Apply Concreteness
Apply concreteness when:
- The sentence is vague or abstract
- The reader cannot easily visualize the idea
- Specific details improve understanding
- The message could lead to multiple interpretations

## Diagnostic Questions
Ask these questions to test concreteness:
- Can the reader picture what is being described?
- Are the nouns specific enough?
- Are the actions clearly defined?
- Would adding a detail reduce ambiguity?
