# Conciseness

## Definition
Conciseness in technical writing means expressing ideas using the fewest words necessary without sacrificing clarity, accuracy, or important details. A concise sentence removes redundancy, wordiness, and unnecessary qualifiers while preserving the original meaning.

Conciseness is not the same as brevity. A longer sentence may be more effective if it includes essential information, while a shorter sentence may be ineffective if it omits critical details.

## Key Principles
- Eliminate redundant or repetitive words.
- Replace wordy phrases with precise terms.
- Avoid unnecessary qualifiers and fillers.
- Do not repeat information that is already implied.

## Common Conciseness Problems

### Redundant Words
Using words that repeat the same meaning.

Examples:
- “two twins”
- “end result”
- “final outcome”
- “past history”

### Wordy Phrases
Using long expressions where a shorter word or phrase would suffice.

Examples:
- “the top of the table” instead of “tabletop”
- “at this point in time” instead of “now”
- “due to the fact that” instead of “because”

### Unnecessary Modifiers
Using adjectives or adverbs that add no new meaning.

Examples:
- “very unique”
- “completely finished”
- “absolutely essential”

### Indirect or Inflated Language
Using overly formal or vague expressions instead of direct language.

## Common Signals of Conciseness Violations
Conciseness should be reviewed when:
- Phrases can be removed without changing meaning
- Multiple words express a single idea
- Formal-sounding phrases replace simple verbs
- Modifiers such as “very,” “possibly,” or “in order to” add no value
- The sentence feels longer than necessary for its purpose

## Examples

### Poor (Redundant)
The two twins work in the same department.

### Improved
The twins work in the same department.

---

### Poor (Redundant Praise)
You are the best outstanding person.

### Improved
You are an outstanding person.

---

### Poor (Wordy Phrase)
Please raise the top of the table another 3 inches.

### Improved
Please raise the tabletop another 3 inches.

---

### Poor (Unnecessary Words)
Our marketing director thinks that perhaps next week or the following one, we will be ready to announce our new scanner.

### Improved
Our marketing director anticipates that within the next two weeks, we will be ready to announce our new scanner.

---

### Poor (Inflated Language)
It is important to note that the meeting will begin at 10 a.m.

### Improved
The meeting will begin at 10 a.m.

---

### Poor (Excessive Wordiness)
At this point in time, the company is in the process of conducting an evaluation of its current procedures in order to determine whether improvements might possibly be implemented in the near future.

### Improved
The company is evaluating its current procedures to determine whether improvements can be implemented.

## When to Apply Conciseness
Apply conciseness when:
- The sentence contains redundant or repeated ideas
- The same meaning can be expressed with fewer words
- A single precise word can replace a phrase
- Extra words do not improve understanding

## Diagnostic Questions
Ask these questions to test conciseness:
- Does any word repeat an idea already implied?
- Can a shorter word or phrase express the same meaning?
- Would removing a word change the meaning of the sentence?
- Is the sentence longer than necessary for its purpose?
