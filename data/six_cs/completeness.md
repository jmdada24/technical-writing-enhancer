# Completeness

## Definition
Completeness in technical writing means providing all necessary information so the reader can fully understand the message and take appropriate action without needing additional clarification or follow-up questions.

A complete message anticipates the reader’s needs and answers all essential questions related to the task, request, or instruction.

## Key Principles
- Include all essential details: who, what, when, where, why, and how.
- Assume the reader has no prior knowledge of the situation.
- Provide enough information for the reader to act correctly the first time.
- Do not rely on shared context or assumptions that the reader may not have.

## Common Completeness Problems

### Missing Time
The message does not specify when an action should occur.

### Missing Place or Location
The reader is told what to do, but not where to do it.

### Missing Method or Procedure
The message does not explain how the action should be performed.

### Missing Responsibility
It is unclear who is expected to perform the action.

### Vague or Incomplete Instructions
The message uses general terms that do not give the reader enough information to respond accurately.

## Common Signals of Completeness Violations
A completeness issue is likely when:
- The reader must ask a follow-up question to act
- Time, place, or method is implied but not stated
- Responsibility is unclear or assumed
- Instructions rely on shared background knowledge
- The message answers “what” but not “how” or “when”

## Examples

### Poor (Missing Details)
Meet me at exactly 2 p.m.

### Improved
Please meet me on Friday, June 15, at exactly 2 p.m. in Room TEC 114 of the GET Building.

---

### Poor (Missing Location)
The training session will begin at 9 a.m.

### Improved
The training session will begin at 9 a.m. in the main conference room on the second floor.

---

### Poor (Missing Responsibility)
The report should be submitted by Friday.

### Improved
The project manager should submit the report by Friday.

---

### Poor (Missing Method)
Submit the form by the deadline.

### Improved
Submit the completed form through the online portal by the stated deadline.

---

### Poor (Incomplete Paragraph)
The orientation session will be held next week. Attendance is required, and materials will be provided.

### Improved
The orientation session will be held on Tuesday, April 9, from 9:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. in Room 204 of the Training Center. All new employees are required to attend, and printed materials will be provided at the venue.

## When to Apply Completeness
Apply completeness when:
- Important details are missing
- The reader cannot act without asking follow-up questions
- Instructions or announcements are unclear or incomplete
- The message assumes background knowledge the reader may not have

## Diagnostic Questions
Ask these questions to test completeness:
- Can the reader act on this message immediately?
- Are time, place, and method clearly stated?
- Is it clear who is responsible for the action?
- Would a new reader understand this without additional context?
